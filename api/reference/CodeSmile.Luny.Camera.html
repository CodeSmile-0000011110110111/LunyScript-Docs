LunyScript API Reference

Namespace CodeSmile.Luny.Camera

Classes

CameraShot

A camera mode and its standard values: the part of a Cinemachine camera's setup that LunyCamera writes when it applies the shot. Everything else on the camera, its targets, its output channel, its extensions and the settings of its components that no value here names, stays as the camera's template or the scene authored it. A shot is immutable: each With call returns a changed copy.

LunyCamera

LunyCamera's entry type: it finds the view a camera, a rig or an object under them belongs to. A view is created or bound by a CameraHost, the one persistent owner, or by a LunyCoop session; the shots and layouts are CameraShot and ViewportLayout, and LunyData.Camera reads both from Luny/Camera/.

LunyCameraException

A mistake in the use of LunyCamera: a second CameraHost, a view created with no template, a layout rectangle outside the unit square, a shot value out of its range, or a null argument. The message names the call and what to change. A refusal the game can expect, such as a rig on another output channel, is a CameraResult instead.

ViewportLayout

Where each view goes for each set of slots in: a table of two viewports for two slots in, placed by order of slot number, and a table of four viewports for three or four slots in, placed by slot number so a slot keeps its cell when another slot empties. One slot in takes the whole screen. The shared view takes the whole screen while nobody is in and the free cell while three slots are in. A slot is a local player's place, 1 to 4, such as LunyCoop's seat number.

Structs

ViewAudience

Who a view shows its picture to: every local player at once, or one player, named by the number the view's creator gave, such as LunyCoop's participant identity. The audience is who sees the view, never what it frames: a shared view aimed at a remote boss still shows to every local player.

ViewId

The identity of one view: a number LunyCamera gives each view it creates or binds and never gives again in the same play session. A view keeps its identity across scene loads while its output exists; a participant who leaves and a guest who takes the seat afterwards have two views with two identities, so a consumer that keeps an identity never mistakes the second for the first.

Viewport

A share of the screen in the unit square: 0 to 1 on both axes with 0 at the bottom left, the way Unity's Camera.rect reads it. A viewport of zero width or height is no share at all.
