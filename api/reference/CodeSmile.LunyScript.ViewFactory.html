The View surface inside Build(): what a local player sees, and which camera rig it renders.

public readonly struct ViewFactory
Expand Details ...

Examples

On.Ready(View.For(Screen).SetCamera(ChaseRig));

Remarks

A view and a camera rig are two objects, not one. The view is the thing that draws part of the screen; the rig is the authored Cinemachine configuration that decides where the shot is. Keeping them separate is what lets one rig serve a group view and four separate views serve four local players, which is the accepted design's own reason for the split.

Both are declared asset inputs, assigned per object in the Inspector. The core cannot name a Camera or a Cinemachine type, so a view and a rig reach the camera adapter as the object a declared input resolved to, and the adapter is what knows whether the object is one. An object that is not is reported and nothing is written.

The camera family is routed through Cinemachine and nothing else. That is the captain's answer on the camera family, 2026-09-13. With the Cinemachine package absent, CodeSmile.LunyScript.Cinemachine does not compile in, no provider is installed, and every block on this surface writes nothing and says so once.

This surface's shape is provisional. It implements the recorded recommendation of the open decision contract-view-routing, which keeps the view separate from its rig and its target group. The alternative that decision holds open makes the player the entry point instead. Nothing below this surface depends on which is chosen.

Examples

On.Ready(View.SplitScreen(View1, View2, View3, View4));

Remarks

The layout. View n is local player n's: the first is the host's, local player 1, whom local player 0 also names, and the others are guests 2 to 4. A player is in while Input.ForPlayer(n).IsPaired reads true, so a player whose controller is lost keeps the view. With nobody in, the shared view Shared(ObjectBinding) names covers the area, or the first view without one. One player in takes the whole area. Two players take the top half, the lower number, and the bottom half. Three or four players take the quarter of their number: 1 top left, 2 top right, 3 bottom left, 4 bottom right, and with three in the shared view takes the quarter nobody holds. Every view with no share gets a viewport of zero width and height, which Unity does not render. The area is the whole screen unless Within(NumberValueBlock, NumberValueBlock, NumberValueBlock, NumberValueBlock) names a part of it.

When. Running the block writes the layout for the players in at that moment, and the runner writes it again at the end of every frame update in which the players in changed, which is the frame a player pairs or leaves. A frame with no change writes nothing.

How long. One split screen is in force at a time. It belongs to the instance that ran the block and ends when that instance ends, when another View.SplitScreen block runs, and when View.For(view).SetViewport writes one of its views. Ending it writes nothing; every view keeps the viewport it has. docs/proposals/split-screen.md is the contract.
