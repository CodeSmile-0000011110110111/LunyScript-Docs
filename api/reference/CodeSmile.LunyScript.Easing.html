Examples

Remarks

The curve turns elapsed time, 0 at the start and 1 at the end of the duration, into progress along the path: 0 is where the object was when the move started and 1 is the destination. Every curve is exactly 0 at the start and exactly 1 at the end, so an eased move still lands on its destination when the duration ends.

These are the 31 standard easing curves. In starts slowly and speeds up, Out starts quickly and slows down, and InOut does both, reaching half the path at half the duration. The families differ in how strongly they bend: Sine, Quad, Cubic, Quart, Quint and Expo in increasing order, and Circ along a quarter circle.

Three families turn back on purpose. Back goes about 10 percent of the path behind the start (InBack) or past the destination (OutBack) before it arrives. Elastic swings past its end several times with shrinking amplitude. Bounce reaches its end and falls back short of it three times, like a dropped ball. A move with these curves travels outside the straight segment between start and destination, or back along it, while it runs.

A rotation follows the same curve along the shortest arc between the two orientations.
